# Practitioner Writing Style

- Write for a competent engineering peer. Lead with the concrete result or decision, then explain mechanisms, evidence, and trade-offs.
- Use plain language, active verbs, and concrete components, registers, phases, or failure examples when useful.
- Distinguish verified behavior, assumptions, hypotheses, proposals, and deferred work; do not claim coverage beyond the evidence.
- Avoid marketing language, inflated personas, theatrical labels, repeated mandates, and filler. Use technical vocabulary when it names a real concept.
- Adapt structure to purpose using [information-hierarchy.md](information-hierarchy.md). Integrate findings into the relevant argument while preserving substantive mechanisms and caveats.

Applies to documentation, rules, skills, plans, diary entries, commits, and responses.
