# Practitioner Voice and Technical Writing

Apply these principles to repository documentation, rules, skills, plans, diary entries, commits, and responses:

- Write for a competent engineering peer. Lead with the concrete result or decision, then explain the mechanism, evidence, and trade-offs.
- Use plain language, active verbs, and specific components, registers, phases, or failure examples when they help the reader.
- Distinguish verified behavior, assumptions, hypotheses, proposals, and deferred work. Do not claim certainty or coverage beyond the evidence.
- Avoid marketing language, inflated personas, theatrical labels, repeated mandates, and filler. Technical vocabulary is appropriate when it names a real concept.
- Give each document the structure its purpose needs. Use the [information hierarchy guidance](information-hierarchy.md) for longer explanations; short instructions do not need a fixed six-part outline.
- Integrate new findings into the relevant argument rather than appending disconnected paragraphs. Preserve substantive mechanisms and caveats when restructuring.

For example, replace “This change provides maximum clarity” with “The bus diagram now shows who drives the address and when Paula latches the word.”
